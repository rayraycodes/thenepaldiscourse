03 — The Nepal Discourse 2027

Four themes for a collective future.

01

Climate

From protest to preparedness

Read moreShow less about Climate

For years, Nepal’s climate message has been a protest: we did little to cause this crisis, yet we feel it most. That is true, but a grievance alone does not protect a single village from a flood or landslide or other climate-induced disasters. Climate disasters strike wealthy nations too. What sets them apart is how ready they are to respond and to care for their people. This theme argues that Nepal’s strongest bargaining power will come from what it builds at home: early warning systems, resilient settlements, restored landscapes, and plans that turn risk into readiness… And how can we scale these solutions globally by leveraging this crisis into opportunities? A Nepal that can show what it has done earns a stronger voice in asking the world to do its part. How do we build that capacity, and how do we bring it to the global table?

02

Cities

The Nepal we want to live in

Read moreShow less about Cities

Nepal is becoming an urban country, and quickly. New municipalities are taking shape, towns are turning into cities, and families are investing their savings in homes and businesses. Most of this growth is happening now, before plans are in place. This theme asks what it would take to plan first: to give every growing city the vision, data, and local authority to shape its own path instead of simply following Kathmandu’s. Federalism promised communities a greater say over their futures, and cities are where that promise becomes real. What does a well-planned Nepali city look like, one that is walkable, safe in an earthquake, rooted in its heritage, and built for the people who live, work, and play there? Most importantly, how can we develop competitive ecosystems beyond Kathmandu?

03

Culture

Who is Nepali?

Read moreShow less about Culture

There may be more ways to be Nepali today than ever before. A teenager in Kathmandu, a worker in Doha, a student in Brisbane, and a grandmother in Jumla each hold part of the answer. This theme invites a conversation about identity in a country of many peoples and a diaspora spread across the world. What do we carry with us, what do we pass on, and what are we creating that is new? How can Nepal’s languages, faiths, cuisines, music, literature, arts, and stories be shared with the world on our own terms? And what role can the diaspora play in shaping how Nepal sees itself and how the world sees Nepal?

04

Capital

How do we make future investments?

Read moreShow less about Capital

Nepal’s greatest export has been its people (young talents and workers in the Middle East for labor). The money they send home keeps families and the national economy going, and it reflects extraordinary sacrifice. But an economy built on leaving cannot be the end goal. As Nepal moves toward middle-income status, this theme asks how to turn that hard-earned capital into opportunities at home. How can remittances and diaspora skills and resources flow into businesses and society, not just households? Which sectors, whether tourism, clean energy, agriculture, or digital work, can create the jobs young Nepalis are leaving to find? How can Nepal attract investment from its neighbors and the wider world on terms that serve its own long-term future? And how can philanthropy and giving (and receiving) be studied and structured in Nepal to build on the inherent generosity of Nepali people around the world?
